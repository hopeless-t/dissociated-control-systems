## Line A — Local sleep/wake state dissociation

**SOURCE**

- [P-DISSOC-2009] Terzaghi et al.
- [P-HDEEG-2016] Castelnovo et al.
- [P-HDEEG-2026] Valomon et al.

**ESTABLISHED SOURCE OBSERVATION**

NREM parasomnia literature contains direct and high-density EEG evidence that
episodes can combine wake-like activity in some systems with sleep-like
activity in others.

**OUR INFERENCE**

A single scalar label such as "awake" or "asleep" can be insufficient for some
episodes. This motivates a vector-valued subsystem state as a mathematical
object.

**EXPERIMENT IMPACT**

~~~text
HYP-002  Subsystem State Dissociation
VAL-001  Latent-State Non-Identifiability
VAL-002  State recovery under partial observation
~~~

## Line B — Complex behavior with amnesia under zolpidem

**SOURCE**

- [P-SRED-2002] Morgenthaler and Silber.
- [R-SRED-2020] Ho et al.
- [I-FDA-CSB] FDA safety communication.

**ESTABLISHED SOURCE OBSERVATION**

Published cases and reviews describe zolpidem-associated complex sleep
behaviors, including eating and meal preparation, together with partial or
complete amnesia in some patients.

**OUR INFERENCE**

Observable behavioral complexity and later episodic recall should be treated as
separate observables.

**EXPERIMENT IMPACT**

VAL-001 deliberately gives coarse behavior and richer state observations
different information content.

## Line C — Paradoxical zolpidem response after brain injury

**SOURCE**

- [R-MESOCIRCUIT-2010] Schiff.
- [P-ZOL-METAB-2014] Chatelle et al.

**ESTABLISHED SOURCE OBSERVATION**

A small subset of patients with disorders of consciousness has shown
paradoxical behavioral improvement after zolpidem. Circuit-level models have
been proposed and small imaging studies in known responders report metabolic
changes.

**OUR INFERENCE**

~~~text
observed low function
!=
proof that all underlying capability is absent
~~~

This phenomenon is clinically and mechanistically distinct from complex sleep
behavior.

**EXPERIMENT IMPACT**

~~~text
HYP-001  Capability–Accessibility Separation
HYP-004  Local Intervention / Global Transition
~~~

## Line D — Partial observability and state-space methods

**SOURCE**

- [M-HMM-1989] Rabiner.
- [S-DYNAMAX] Dynamax.
- [S-HMMLEARN] hmmlearn.
- [S-SSM] lindermanlab/ssm.

**ESTABLISHED METHOD**

Hidden-state and state-space models represent systems where observations are
generated by latent states with explicit transition structure.

**OUR INFERENCE**

These methods provide a natural formal vocabulary for latent subsystem state,
transition, intervention, observation, and posterior ambiguity.

**EXPERIMENT IMPACT**

VAL-001 starts without probabilistic inference so the known-answer ambiguity is
transparent. VAL-002 may compare inference families only after synthetic ground
truth is frozen.

## Line E — Engineered-systems analogy

**SOURCE**

No neuroscience source supports direct transfer to computer systems.

**OUR INFERENCE**

A useful engineered-system formalism might separately model physical
capability, access gate, active state, observer, recorder, and controller.

**EXPERIMENT IMPACT**

Cross-domain comparison remains downstream of domain-local validation.

~~~text
Analogy != evidence of shared mechanism
~~~


## Line F — Voice/speech as a partial observation of cognitive-control state

**SOURCE**

- [P-VOICE-COG-2026] Russo, Hawkshaw, and Sataloff.
- [P-VOICE-SCREEN-2026] Mekulu, Aqlan, and Yang.

**ESTABLISHED SOURCE OBSERVATION**

A large retrospective health-record study reports an association between
diagnosed voice disorders and later incident cognitive decline. A separate
DementiaBank study reports that a sparse, interpretable model using short
picture-description speech/transcript features can classify dementia cases and
controls with useful held-out discrimination in that dataset.

These findings concern different observables and study designs. They must not be
collapsed into one biomarker claim.

**OUR INFERENCE**

Voice and speech are candidate low-burden observation channels for a latent
control-state model. Their value may be strongest longitudinally, especially
when combined with an independent task-performance channel and explicit nuisance
modeling.

Deliberate speech modulation is also a useful adversarial case: if the voice
channel can be changed without independent cognitive change, the model must not
mistake sensor gaming for latent-state recovery.

**EXPERIMENT IMPACT**

~~~text
RQ-VOICE-001  Voice as a longitudinal control-state sensor
HYP-005       Longitudinal Voice Observability
HYP-006       Volitional State Perturbation
HYP-007       Sensor-Gaming Separability
~~~
